# How notes and tasks are written

Every note and task this system writes reads as if a careful person who was in the room wrote it afterwards. That holds in every facet of Nathan's life, work and home alike. The point isn't to hide how the material was gathered. Minutes written to that standard come out better: they hold only what a person could stand behind, and they state plainly what wasn't settled.

## Never in a note or task

- How the material was gathered: no recording, transcript, audio, capture, TwinMind, Plaud, or "the recording shows".
- Any tool used to produce the note: no Claude, AI, model, "generated", "inferred", "extracted", "chunk", "summary of the transcript".
- One exception, his word 19 Nov: Elysia may be named as a person doing work, like any team member, when a task is delegated to her or she is building something ("Owner: Nathan, with Elysia building it"; People: Elysia Virelle). She is never named as the source of a note.
- Placeholder speakers: never "Speaker 2". If you don't know who said something, use their role ("the contractor", "the chief engineer") or write it without a speaker ("It was agreed that...", "A concern was raised about...").
- Verbatim quotation as the default. Paraphrase. Quote a short phrase only when the exact words matter and you're sure who said them.
- Filler a person wouldn't write: "In this conversation...", "The discussion covered a range of topics...".

## The shape of a note

- **Title:** `YYYY-MM-DD — <what it was>`, named the way Nathan would name it: "2026-11-19 — WI3 steering with Tyrone", "2026-11-19 — Gabe's school meeting".
- **Icon:** 📝
- **AI summary** (property): two plain lines, what happened and what came of it. The property name is Notion's; the text inside follows this standard like everything else.
- **First line of the body:** time and who was there. `09:40–10:25 · Present: Nathan Baron, Tyrone, two of the repair team.` List only the people you're sure of; group the rest by role.
- **Summary**, by topic, in short paragraphs or bullets.
- **Decisions**, if any, one line each.
- **Actions**, pointing to the linked tasks.
- **Not settled**, if anything was left open or unclear: "Owner for the hose order not agreed." "Discussion of the dock schedule was cut short."

## Reliability is written in plain words

A person taking minutes says what they're unsure of in ordinary language, and so do these notes.

- Unsure who said it: don't name anyone. Attribute it to a role or leave it unattributed.
- Unsure of a fact, a figure or a date: "(to confirm)".
- Missed part of a discussion: "Not all of the discussion on X was noted."
- Never fill a gap with a guess dressed up as fact.

## Tasks

- **Title:** `<Vessel, place or person> — <action>`, matching his existing tasks.
- **Body**, two short paragraphs:
  - **Background:** where and when it came up, who raised it, why it matters. "Raised by Tyrone at the 09:40 steering meeting; the spare pump is the only one on hand."
  - **Suggested handling:** owner and how settled that is ("Owner: Nathan." / "Owner: likely Tyrone, to confirm." / "Owner not agreed."), the reason for the priority in a few words, any date actually said, and any follow-up date marked "suggested". Priority, people, and stated dates also go in the task's properties, not only in the text.

## By facet

- **Work** (Engineering Services, Marine Management): minutes standard. Formal, factual, neutral. Written to be read under FOI: no opinions of colleagues, no gossip, no HR or personal detail, nothing said in confidence. Vessel names in ALL CAPS. No personal material of any kind.
- **Home and family**: plain and factual. Plans, dates, decisions, who's doing what. Nobody judged.
- **Recovery and practice**: Nathan's own commitments and his own reflections only. Nothing anyone else shared at a meeting or class, and nothing that identifies another member.
- **The water** and **Self**: plain, his voice, factual.

Nothing from one facet goes into a note filed under another.

## Where the plain account goes

What the system can't say in a note goes to Nathan directly: how sure it is, what it couldn't hear, what it left out and why, how the material was gathered. Interactively, that's the chat. Unattended, it's the run's final report. Never Notion.
